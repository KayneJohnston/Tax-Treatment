## 6.1 Legal certainty ratings (§23)

The brief rightly insists on the distinction between **"the law appears to permit this"** and
**"a tax adviser would be comfortable signing off on this."** The ratings below use the second
standard.

| Proposition | Rating | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Bitcoin is a CGT asset and is caught by CGT event I1 on ceasing residency | 🟢 **High** | s 108‑5, s 104‑160; ATO crypto guidance works an explicit Bitcoin example |
| Bitcoin held personally is **not** taxable Australian property | 🟢 **High** | s 855‑15 table is exhaustive; ATO crypto guidance confirms by clear implication |
| A foreign resident's disposal of Bitcoin is disregarded absent the s 104‑165 choice | 🟢 **High** | s 855‑10 |
| The full 50% discount applies to the I1 gain | 🟢 **High** | Discount testing period ends at the I1 event, during all of which the taxpayer was a resident |
| The s 104‑165 choice makes Bitcoin deemed TAP and preserves Australia's taxing rights | 🟢 **High** | s 104‑165(3); ATO crypto guidance says so expressly |
| Making the choice switches off the s 855‑45 cost-base reset on return | 🟢 **High** | **ATO ID 2009/148** — though this is an interpretative decision, not a public ruling |
| s 855‑45 resets the cost base to market value on resuming residency (no choice made) | 🟢 **High** | s 855‑45; "Parts 3‑1 and 3‑3 apply as if you had acquired it" restarts the discount clock too |
| Australia has **no** temporary non-residence clawback rule | 🟢 **High** | Absence of any such provision; contrast UK FA 2013 Sch 45 Pt 4 |
| Remitting sale proceeds to Australia is not a taxable event | 🟢 **High** | No remittance basis in Australian law; only Div 775 forex movement after resumption |
| Timing departure to the start of the income year is effective and lawful | 🟢 **High** | The gain falls in the year of the event; part-year threshold rules are express |
| A genuine relocation is not caught by Part IVA | 🟢→🟡 **High, fact-dependent** | Objective purpose test; genuineness of the move is decisive |
| **This taxpayer will succeed in ceasing Australian residency** | 🟡 **Reasonably defensible, entirely fact-dependent** | No bright lines (TR 2023/1). *Quy* is a warning; *Harding* is the template. **Execution decides this, not law** |
| Moving crypto between wallets you control is not a CGT event | 🟢 **High** | ATO: beneficial ownership is the test |

## 6.2 Substance and residency risk (§24)

### What the ATO would argue: *"You never actually ceased Australian tax residency"*

Every one of these is a real argument the Commissioner has run and won:

| Fact | The ATO's argument | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Spouse remained in Australia** | Your centre of personal relations is Australia; you are physically absent, not resident elsewhere. **This was decisive in *Quy*** | 🔴 Fatal |
| **Children remained in Australia** | Same. Family is the strongest single indicator of where a person "resides" | 🔴 Fatal |
| **Australian home retained and available** | You have a permanent place of abode in Australia. **Explicitly relied on in *Quy*** — the family home "remained available to him" | 🔴 Fatal |
| **Returning frequently** | Your presence in Australia is usual and settled, not casual | 🟠 High |
| **Continuing Australian employment / secondment** | Your presence abroad is a work assignment, not a change of home. **Central in *Quy*** | 🔴 Fatal |
| **Foreign visa tied to employer sponsorship** | You have no independent right to remain; your foreign abode is not permanent. **Relied on in *Quy*** | 🟠 High |
| **Australian social ties** (clubs, sporting bodies, community roles) | Australia remains your community | 🟡 Moderate |
| **Australian business interests actively managed** | Economic centre remains in Australia | 🟠 High |
| **Australian driver's licence retained** | Minor, but cumulative | 🟢 Low |
| **Medicare enrolment retained** | You held yourself out as an Australian resident to another agency | 🟡 Moderate |
| **Australian bank accounts as primary banking** | Financial life still centred in Australia | 🟡 Moderate |
| **Time in Australia** | Approaching 183 days engages a separate statutory test | 🟠 High |
| **Weak ties to the destination** | No permanent place of abode outside Australia (domicile test fails) | 🔴 Fatal |
| **Return shortly after the disposal** | The absence was a device; look at the whole arrangement | 🟠 High |

### Hypothetical STRONG case 🟢

> The taxpayer resigns from their Australian employer effective 30 June. The family home is sold
> in May. On 8 July the taxpayer, spouse and child fly to Dubai on a Golden Visa obtained through
> a property purchase in the taxpayer's own name. They move into that property. The child starts
> Year 2 at a Dubai school in September and continues there for six years. The spouse obtains
> local part-time work in year 2. The taxpayer takes a genuine local-hire role with a UAE-domiciled
> employer in year 1. They obtain Emirates IDs, UAE driver's licences, local health insurance, and
> local bank accounts used for all day-to-day spending. Medicare enrolment is withdrawn and an
> Entitlement Statement obtained. They obtain a UAE tax residency certificate every year. They
> return to Australia for 14–20 days each year to see family, staying with parents or in hotels.
> They own no Australian residential property. They join a Dubai sports club, and the child has a
> local friendship group. In year 7 the Bitcoin is sold through a VARA-licensed venue into a UAE
> bank account. In year 8, after the taxpayer's mother becomes ill, the family returns to Australia.
>
> **Assessment: 🟢 very strong. On these facts the ATO's residency argument fails on both the
> ordinary-concepts and domicile tests. This is the *Harding* pattern.**

### Hypothetical WEAK case 🔴

> The taxpayer takes a two-year secondment to Dubai with their existing Australian employer, who
> continues to pay them and sponsors their visa. The spouse and child remain in Melbourne so the
> child can finish primary school. The family home is retained, unlet, with the taxpayer's
> belongings in it. The taxpayer returns to Melbourne for two weeks every second month — about 90
> days a year — and stays in the family home. Australian bank accounts, Medicare, private health
> cover, driver's licence and gym membership are all retained. The taxpayer rents a serviced
> apartment in Dubai. Eighteen months in, the Bitcoin is sold. Three months later the secondment
> ends and the taxpayer returns to Melbourne.
>
> **Assessment: 🔴 fails. This is *Quy*, with an added Part IVA overlay because the disposal was
> timed to the absence. Expect a full assessment on the gain plus penalties.**

### The exposure if residency fails

| Component | Amount |
|---|---|
| Australian CGT on the $2.8m gain as a resident (discounted, on top of ~$150k foreign salary) | **$654,800** |
| Less: refund of the I1 tax paid (I1 never happened if residency never ceased) | ($192,400) |
| Australian tax on 7 years of foreign salary assessed as a resident (no foreign tax credits from a 0% jurisdiction) | ≈**$277,000** |
| Shortfall penalty at 25% (failure to take reasonable care) to 50% (recklessness) | **$164,000 – $328,000** |
| Shortfall interest charge over a 3-year assessment lag | ≈**$230,000** |
| **Indicative total exposure** | **≈$1.13m – $1.30m** |

> **This — not the $192,400 — is the number that should drive every substance decision.** The
> difference between the strong case and the weak case above is roughly **$1.3 million** and costs
> nothing but doing the relocation properly.

## 6.3 Failure-mode analysis (§30)

Ten ways the recommended strategy could fail, with probability (given competent execution of the
strong case), financial impact, and mitigation.

### 1. The ATO determines the taxpayer remained an Australian resident
- **Probability: 15%** (strong case) / **75%** (weak case)
- **Impact: ≈$1.13m–$1.30m** (see above)
- **Mitigation:** relocate the whole family; sell or genuinely let the Australian home; resign
  Australian employment and take a genuine local hire; obtain independent residency rights (Golden
  Visa, not employer sponsorship); keep Australian visits under ~45 days a year; obtain a foreign
  TRC every year; build the contemporaneous evidence file from day one; prefer a treaty country if
  the family is willing, so an Article 4 tie-breaker is available as a backstop.

### 2. The destination does not recognise the taxpayer as tax resident
- **Probability: 10%**
- **Impact:** Does not itself create Australian tax, but severely undermines the *Harding*
  "permanent place of abode outside Australia" argument, feeding failure mode 1. Also risks the
  destination taxing nothing while Australia taxes everything.
- **Mitigation:** target the UAE **183-day** test, not the 90-day route. Apply for and obtain a TRC
  in year 1 and every year after. Keep a contemporaneous day-count log with boarding passes.

### 3. CGT event I1 creates an unexpected Australian liability
- **Probability: ~100% — this is a certainty, not a risk.** The real risk is **illiquidity**: a
  ≈$192,400 bill with no sale.
- **Impact: $192,400** (plus general interest charge if unpaid, currently ~11% p.a.)
- **Mitigation:** fund it deliberately — sell the oldest, highest-cost-base parcels before
  departure, or take a low-LTV BTC-backed loan. Model the departure-year return **before** choosing
  the departure date. Do not discover this in October of the following year.

### 4. The taxpayer makes the s 104‑165 choice
- **Probability: 20%** if unadvised (it is the intuitive choice — "defer the tax") / **<2%** if advised
- **Impact: +$752,200** against the recommended route, and **worse than never leaving**
- **Mitigation:** put it in writing to the tax agent **before** lodgement. Check the lodged return.
  The choice is **irrevocable** — there is no second chance.

### 5. The destination treats the sale as ordinary income, not capital
- **Probability: 5%** (UAE) / **12%** (Singapore, Hong Kong, Malaysia)
- **Impact:** UAE 9% corporate tax above AED 375,000 if the activity is a "business" with turnover
  over AED 1m → up to **~$250,000** on a $3m disposal. Singapore up to 24%, Hong Kong 15%,
  Malaysia up to 30% → **$400,000–$840,000**
- **Mitigation:** hold, do not trade. One disposal, or a small number of tranches over days, not a
  pattern of activity. No leverage, no market-making, no managing others' money, no crypto business
  licence. Document the long-term investment intent from acquisition. Obtain a local ruling if the
  amount justifies it.

### 6. The taxpayer returns to Australia too soon
- **Probability: 20%** (life happens — illness, jobs, homesickness, a child's schooling)
- **Impact:** No *statutory* consequence, but it materially strengthens the ATO's residency
  argument, feeding failure mode 1.
- **Mitigation:** plan for 5–7 years minimum. If circumstances force an early return, **document the
  reason contemporaneously** — a genuine, evidenced change of circumstances is a complete answer to
  the "you always intended to return" inference. Do not sell the Bitcoin and then return within
  months.

### 7. Australian family ties undermine non-residency
- **Probability: 40%** if the spouse or child stays; **<10%** if the family relocates fully
- **Impact:** effectively equals failure mode 1 — **$1.13m–$1.30m**
- **Mitigation:** the family relocates. There is no partial version of this that works. If the
  spouse will not move, **do not do this strategy** — the analysis changes completely and the
  do-nothing base case at $231,800 becomes the right answer.

### 8. A crypto transaction triggers CGT before departure
- **Probability: 25%** (this is easier to do by accident than people expect)
- **Impact:** a swap, wrap or stablecoin conversion of the full position while resident realises
  the $1m gain at resident rates — **$231,800** — with no offsetting benefit, and it can push the
  gain into a year with full salary
- **Mitigation:** a written "do not touch" rule for 12 months before departure. No wrapping (ATO
  draft **TD 2026/D2**, 19 August 2026: wrapping and unwrapping are each CGT events), no
  crypto-to-crypto, no stablecoin conversion, no DeFi lending, no bridging, no ETF conversion. Only
  transfers between wallets you control — and remember the **network fee paid in BTC is itself a
  disposal**.

### 9. A foreign exit tax applies
- **Probability: <5%** (UAE, Singapore, Hong Kong, Malaysia, Switzerland, Cayman — none has an
  individual exit tax) / **~100%** if Portugal is chosen and Bitcoin is still held on departure
- **Impact:** Portugal — deemed disposal at market value on ceasing Portuguese residency (though
  the ≥365-day holding exclusion should apply). Japan, Spain and Italy have exit taxes and are
  excluded on rate grounds anyway.
- **Mitigation:** choose a jurisdiction with no exit tax; sell **before** ceasing destination
  residency, not after.

### 10. Australian residency on return produces unexpected CGT
- **Probability: <5%** if no s 104‑165 choice was made; **~100%** if it was and the Bitcoin is still held
- **Impact:** if the choice was made, s 855‑45 is switched off (**ATO ID 2009/148**), the $200,000
  cost base survives, and the entire accumulated gain is eventually taxed in Australia
- **Mitigation:** do not make the choice; sell before returning; document the market value of every
  remaining asset on the resumption date.

### Additional failure modes worth tracking

| # | Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | **Australia legislates a temporary non-residence rule** or enacts the Board of Taxation residency model unfavourably | 15% over 10 years | Could retrospectively defeat the strategy for those still holding | Sell earlier rather than later; monitor Treasury consultations |
| 12 | **Foreign resident CGT regime expansion** (exposure drafts of 10 April 2026) extends to crypto | 10% | Would make the s 104‑165 trap worse; should not affect a crystallised position | Crystallise I1 — a completed transaction is far harder to reach |
| 13 | **Valuation dispute** over the I1 market value | 10% | ±$50,000 of tax | Two-venue timestamped valuation; documented methodology; consider a formal valuation |
| 14 | **Banking or exchange freeze** on liquidation | 25% of *some* delay | Timing risk, market risk during a hold | Onboard years early; tranche the sale; complete the SoF pack in advance |
| 15 | **MLI PPT / Part IVA** applied to the aggressive route | 40%+ if attempted | Up to $944,600 | Do not attempt the aggressive route |
| 16 | **Loss of the main residence exemption** on the Australian home | 60% if the home is kept and sold while non-resident | Potentially several hundred thousand dollars | Sell as a resident, or only after resuming residency |
| 17 | **Forced liquidation of BTC loan collateral while still Australian resident** | 10% at high LTV | Realises the gain at resident rates at the worst possible time | LTV ≤25%; overcollateralise; use a lender with no rehypothecation |

## 6.4 Twenty questions for an Australian international-tax specialist

Designed to test whether the strategy is **actually safe to execute**, not to elicit general
commentary.

1. On my specific facts — spouse and child relocating with me, Australian home sold, Australian
   employment resigned, UAE Golden Visa in my own name, X days of Australian presence per year —
   **on which precise date do you consider I cease to be an Australian resident under each of the
   four s 6(1) tests**, and what is your confidence level for each?

2. Would you sign a **reasonably arguable position paper** on that cessation date? If not, what
   additional facts would you need?

3. Can I obtain an **ATO private binding ruling** on the CGT consequences of my ceasing residency
   on a stated date, and what is the practical risk that applying for one triggers a residency
   review?

4. **Confirm in writing that I should NOT make the s 104‑165 choice**, and confirm you have checked
   the lodged return to ensure the choice was not inadvertently made. What is your firm's process
   for preventing that error?

5. Please verify against the instrument itself — not commentary — whether the **Australia–[destination]
   treaty's alienation-of-property article contains a residual paragraph** conferring exclusive
   residence-state taxing rights over "any other property", and whether it contains a
   **former-resident preservation clause** and, if so, its exact lookback period and starting point.

6. Do you agree that **ATO ID 2009/148** means the s 855‑45 cost-base reset is unavailable on
   re-entry for assets subject to a s 104‑165 choice? Is there any contrary authority, and has the
   ATO's position changed since 2009?

7. Confirm that **Australia has no temporary non-residence rule** equivalent to UK FA 2013 Sch 45
   Pt 4, and that nothing in the 2026 foreign resident CGT exposure drafts, the Board of Taxation
   residency model, or any announced measure would introduce one.

8. What **discount percentage** applies to my CGT event I1 gain, and can you confirm from my
   acquisition records that the discount testing period contains no foreign-resident days?

9. If I cease residency on 10 July, what is my **exact modelled departure-year liability**,
   including the part-year tax-free threshold, the Medicare levy exemption for the foreign-resident
   portion, and the Medicare levy surcharge? Please prepare a draft return.

10. What is the **latest date I can pay** the I1 liability without general interest charge, and is
    a payment arrangement available if I would otherwise have to sell Bitcoin into a falling market?

11. Is a **BTC-backed loan with [named lender]** a genuine security arrangement within **s 106‑60**,
    such that no CGT event arises on pledging? Please review that lender's actual terms for
    rehypothecation rights. What happens for CGT purposes if they liquidate my collateral?

12. Does **draft TD 2026/D2** (wrapped tokens) or any other current ATO position affect my holdings
    if I do nothing but transfer between wallets I control? Are network fees paid in BTC disposals,
    and how should I record them?

13. Assess my **Part IVA exposure**, applying each of the eight s 177D(2) factors to my facts. What
    is the identified scheme, the tax benefit, and the counterfactual the Commissioner would run?

14. If the ATO later challenges my residency, what **shortfall penalty** would you expect (25%, 50%
    or 75%) and what steps now would support a "reasonable care" position? Should I obtain a
    contemporaneous written opinion for that purpose?

15. What are my ongoing **Australian lodgement obligations** as a non-resident — Australian-source
    income, HELP repayment on worldwide income, the overseas travel notification — and what are the
    consequences of getting them wrong?

16. If I keep my Australian home and let it out, quantify the **main residence exemption denial**,
    the foreign resident CGT withholding, the discount apportionment, and any state land tax
    surcharge exposure as an Australian citizen abroad.

17. On my return, what evidence will the ATO accept for the **s 855‑45 market value** of each asset
    on the resumption date, and how long must I keep it?

18. If I hold sale proceeds in USD and convert to AUD after resuming residency, quantify the
    **Division 775** exposure, and confirm that converting before resumption puts the currency
    movement outside the Australian net.

19. Are there any **CARF or domestic crypto reporting** obligations from 2027 that will attach to me
    as a non-resident with an Australian nexus, and what will the ATO see about my disposal?

20. **What would you do differently?** Specifically: is there any step in this plan you would not
    put your name to, and is there anything materially better that I have not considered?

*(For the destination adviser, the parallel list should include: the exact residency test I will
satisfy and the evidence required; whether my disposal is capital or business income under local
law; whether local turnover or business-activity thresholds could be engaged; whether any exit tax
applies when I leave; whether the local cost base is my original cost or a stepped-up value; and
whether a local advance ruling is available.)*

## 6.5 Red flags

Everything found in this research that makes the apparent 0%-tax strategy **less attractive than
it first appears**.

1. **You cannot escape tax on the existing gain. Full stop.** CGT event I1 taxes the $1m embedded
   gain on the way out. The strategy protects **future** appreciation only. If Bitcoin goes
   sideways, the whole exercise saves $39,400 and costs far more than that to execute.

2. **The intuitive "defer the tax" election is the single worst move available** — worse than never
   leaving Australia, in every price scenario modelled, by $89,700 to $1,067,700. It is
   irrevocable. Most people who move overseas with crypto and hear "you can elect to defer" will
   take it.

3. **Australia's effective CGT rate on a long-held asset is ~22.6%, not 47%.** The 50% discount
   makes the do-nothing base case far less painful than the headline rate suggests. The hurdle to
   beat is low; so is the prize.

4. ***Quy* [2025] ARTA 174 is a Dubai case decided against the taxpayer.** The ATO has recent,
   successful litigation experience with exactly this fact pattern, and won even though the
   taxpayer spent under two months a year in Australia.

5. **No Australia–UAE tax treaty exists, and none is being negotiated.** The best-ranked
   destination offers no Article 4 tie-breaker. If the ATO wins the domestic residency argument,
   there is no second line of defence. The same is true of Hong Kong.

6. **The spouse and child must genuinely move.** This is not a tax structuring point that can be
   engineered around; it is a lived-life requirement, and it is where most such plans fail.

7. **The UAE Golden Visa property requirement (AED 2m ≈ AUD 830k) is ~70% of this taxpayer's net
   worth**, and funding it requires selling Bitcoin — the very thing the strategy exists to avoid
   selling in Australia. This is a genuine chicken-and-egg problem.

8. **Several famous "0% crypto countries" are not.** Cyprus imposed 8% from 1 January 2026. Italy
   went to 33% in 2026. New Zealand taxes crypto disposals as ordinary income at up to 39% despite
   "having no CGT". Andorra charges 10%. Advice more than 12 months old on this topic is unsafe.

9. **The UK's four-year FIG regime is useless for crypto**, because HMRC treats crypto as situated
   where its beneficial owner resides — so a UK resident's crypto gains are *UK* gains and cannot
   be "foreign". Anyone reading FIG marketing material will get this wrong.

10. **Portugal has a crypto exit tax** and a denial rule for out-of-network counterparties, and the
    Australia–Portugal treaty signed in November 2023 **is still not in force** as at September 2026.

11. **Switzerland's tax-free treatment is a five-criterion safe harbour that a BTC-backed loan
    breaks.** "No use of borrowed capital" is one of the cumulative conditions in Circular 36. And
    Switzerland levies an annual wealth tax on the full Bitcoin value — up to ~$30,000 a year on a
    $3m position.

12. **Singapore, the best all-round destination on tax and banking, is probably closed to this
    taxpayer.** GIP requires SGD 10m; ONE Pass requires SGD 30,000/month. Without a genuine
    Singapore job offer, there is no route in.

13. **Full transparency is coming.** Australia implements CARF from 2027 with first exchanges in
    2028, plus a domestic crypto reporting regime; the ATO's data-matching program already covers
    up to 1.2 million accounts a year; the UAE participates in CRS. The strategy must be correct,
    because it will certainly be visible.

14. **Source-of-funds review is a real operational risk.** Onboarding for a USD 1m+ liquidation
    takes 4–8 weeks and can stall. A frozen account during a drawdown is a material financial risk
    that has nothing to do with tax.

15. **Losing the Australian main residence exemption can cost more than the entire crypto plan
    saves.** A foreign resident vendor loses it *entirely* — not pro-rata — and the "life events"
    exception requires foreign residency of six years or less and does not cover ordinary emigration.

16. **No structure helps with the existing gain.** Spouse transfers, trusts, companies and
    partnerships all trigger a market-value disposal now; an SMSF cannot legally acquire crypto
    from a member (SIS Act s 66). The only genuine reducer is concessional super — worth ~$29,750,
    locked up for 26 years.

17. **Capital losses are worth about half what people expect** against a discounted gain — roughly
    22.6c in the dollar.

18. **Total non-tax cost is easily $150,000–$400,000+** over five years — schooling, housing,
    forgone salary, professional fees, relocation — against a modelled tax advantage of $462,400 in
    the base case. **The margin is thinner than the headline suggests.**

19. **The law will change over a 5–10 year horizon.** The 2026 foreign resident CGT exposure drafts
    include provisions applying retrospectively to CGT events from **12 December 2006**. Australia
    is willing to legislate retrospectively in this area.

20. **Australia has not legislated the residency reform.** Planning must be done against fact-heavy,
    unpredictable tests with no bright lines — which is precisely why *Quy* and *Harding* reached
    opposite results on superficially similar facts.

## 6.6 Confidence

| Dimension | Confidence | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Legal confidence** — that the statutory analysis (ss 104‑160, 104‑165, 855‑10, 855‑15, 855‑45, 115‑105/115‑115, 106‑60, 102‑5) is correctly stated | **85%** | The provisions and their operation are corroborated across multiple independent sources including ATO guidance and an ATO interpretative decision. Deducted for the network limitation: **no statute was read first-hand**, and the s 104‑165(3)/s 855‑45 interaction rests on a 2009 ATO ID whose currency was not independently verified |
| **Tax confidence** — that the modelled numbers are approximately correct | **82%** | 2026–27 rates verified from multiple sources; arithmetic checked. Deducted for: the part-year Medicare levy treatment is approximated; Medicare levy surcharge, HELP repayments and offsets are excluded; the s 115‑115 apportionment assumes a stylised acquisition date |
| **Residency confidence** — that this taxpayer would successfully cease Australian residency | **70%** on the strong-case facts; **20%** on the weak-case facts | No bright-line test exists. *Quy* shows the ATO winning on adjacent facts; *Harding* shows a taxpayer winning. **Outcome depends on execution, not on law.** The absence of a UAE treaty tie-breaker removes the backstop |
| **Practical confidence** — that the plan can actually be executed end-to-end | **65%** | Visa, schooling, banking, exchange onboarding, a $3m liquidation, a spouse's career and seven years of family life all have to work. The AED 2m Golden Visa funding problem is unresolved on the stated net worth. Most plans of this kind fail on execution, not on tax law |

### Overall

The **tax law is clearer than most people assume**, and the answer to the brief's central
hypothesis is **yes — the strategy works**, by virtue of s 855‑10, s 855‑45 and the absence of any
Australian temporary non-residence rule.

**The strategy does not fail on law. It fails on facts, or it fails on the s 104‑165 choice.** Get
the family genuinely relocated, do not tick the box, keep the evidence, and the outcome is
defensible. Get either wrong and the downside is between $752,200 and $1.3 million.
