# 2 — Destination jurisdiction screening and deep dives

Covers brief sections 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 27 and 28.

## 2.1 The screening criteria

Fifteen essential criteria from the brief, restated as pass/fail tests:

1. Realistic residency pathway for a 34-year-old with a spouse, a young child and ~AUD 1.2m of net worth
2. Personal tax on Bitcoin investment gains at or near **0%**
3. No wealth tax large enough to defeat the strategy
4. No exit tax capturing the Bitcoin on eventual departure
5. No deemed-disposal regime creating a surprise bill on arrival
6. No tax on offshore capital gains that undermines the plan
7. No requirement to dispose of Bitcoin before obtaining residency
8. No requirement to hold Bitcoin through a local company for favourable treatment
9. Lawful self-custody and access to reputable exchanges
10. Practical ability to move and liquidate large legitimate crypto holdings
11. No rule taxing gains merely because the asset was acquired while Australian resident
12. Strong treaty position with Australia where relevant
13. Reasonable certainty of being recognised as tax resident
14. No problematic exit taxation on return to Australia
15. Political and legal stability

**A structural point that reframes the whole exercise.** If the recommended strategy is followed —
crystallise CGT event I1, do **not** make the s 104‑165 choice — then the *Australian* tax outcome
is **identical regardless of destination**, because after the I1 event the Bitcoin is an ordinary
non-TAP asset and s 855‑10 disregards every subsequent gain. The destination therefore matters
for four things only:

- whether **it** taxes the disposal;
- whether an Australia–destination **DTA tie-breaker** exists as a safety net if the ATO disputes
  residency;
- whether residency there is **genuinely obtainable and defensible**; and
- whether living there works for a family that intends to come home.

Criterion 12 is a **backstop**, not a driver — which is why the UAE can rank first despite having
no treaty with Australia.

### Jurisdictions that have recently changed their crypto taxation

This is the part of the brief most likely to catch out a taxpayer relying on older material:

- **Cyprus** — 8% flat crypto tax from **1 January 2026**. Previously widely marketed as 0%.
- **Italy** — 26% → **33%** from 2026; the €2,000 exemption threshold abolished.
- **Portugal** — NHR closed to new entrants from 1 January 2024; replacement **IFICI** regime is
  narrow (science/R&D/innovation) and offers a passive crypto investor nothing.
- **Thailand** — Por 161/2566 changed the remittance rule from 1 January 2024; the 2025 Royal
  Decree proposal to exempt income remitted within two tax years is **still not enacted**.
- **Indonesia** — MOF Regulation 50/2025 raised the final crypto transaction tax to 0.21% (1% for
  foreign platforms) from 1 August 2025.
- **Malaysia** — foreign-source income exemption for resident individuals extended to
  **31 December 2036**.
- **Oman** — Royal Decree 56/2025 introduces the Gulf's first personal income tax (5%) from
  **1 January 2028**.
- **UK** — remittance basis abolished 6 April 2025, replaced by the four-year FIG regime, which
  by reason of the crypto situs rule does not assist crypto holders.
- **Australia** — CARF and a domestic crypto reporting regime announced 17 December 2025
  (MYEFO), operating from 2027; draft TD 2026/D2 on wrapped tokens issued 19 August 2026;
  exposure drafts strengthening the foreign-resident CGT regime released 10 April 2026.

## 2.3 UAE deep dive

**Verdict: 🟢 the recommended destination — but not because it is famous. Because it is the only
jurisdiction that scores highly on tax outcome, residency obtainability *and* family practicality
simultaneously.**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Personal income tax | **None.** The UAE has no personal income tax law |
| Individual capital gains tax | **None** |
| Crypto investment gains | **0%.** No tax on personal crypto gains for natural persons |
| Corporate tax | 9% above AED 375,000, under Federal Decree-Law No. 47 of 2022 — but it applies to **businesses**, not personal investment |
| **Can personal investing become "business activity"?** | **Yes — this is the one real risk.** Art. 11 of Decree-Law 47 brings a natural person within corporate tax if they conduct a business in the UAE with turnover above **AED 1,000,000** (Cabinet Decision 49/2023). Personal investment income of a natural person is carved out, but **high-frequency trading is not personal investment** |
| Trading vs passive investment | A single disposal of a long-held position is passive investment. **Do not day-trade, run a fund, manage others' money, or operate a licensed crypto business** |
| Offshore assets | Not taxed — there is no tax to apply |
| Wealth / inheritance / estate tax | **None.** (Note: Sharia forced-heirship can apply to UAE-situs assets on death unless a DIFC/ADGM will is registered — an estate-planning point, not a tax point) |
| Exit taxation | **None** |
| **DTA with Australia** | **None.** CEPA (trade) and the Investment Agreement entered into force 1 October 2025 — neither is a tax treaty, and no DTA negotiations have been announced |
| Information exchange with Australia | **Yes — the UAE is a CRS participant**, and will implement CARF |

### Tax residency

Governed by **Cabinet Decision No. 85 of 2022** (effective 1 March 2023) and **Ministerial
Decision No. 27 of 2023**. A natural person is a UAE tax resident if **any** of:

1. Their **usual or primary place of residence** and **centre of financial and personal interests**
   are in the UAE; **or**
2. Physically present in the UAE for **183 days or more** in any consecutive 12 months; **or**
3. Physically present for **90 days or more** in any consecutive 12 months **and** is a UAE or GCC
   national or holds a valid UAE Residence Permit, **and** either has a **permanent place of
   residence** in the UAE or carries on employment or business in the UAE.

All days or **parts of days** of physical presence count, and they need not be consecutive.
"Permanent place of residence" is defined in MD 27/2023 as a dwelling with a "continuous right of
occupation… at all times and on a regular basis with some degree of permanency and stability".

**Recommendation: target the 183-day test, not the 90-day test.** The 90-day route is legally
sufficient for a UAE Tax Residency Certificate, but 90 days in the UAE leaves 275 days elsewhere,
which is precisely the pattern that loses an Australian residency argument. **Live there.**

### Tax Residency Certificate

Issued by the Federal Tax Authority. Because there is no Australia–UAE DTA, the taxpayer can only
obtain a **domestic** TRC (for UAE domestic-law purposes), not a treaty TRC referable to
Australia. A domestic TRC is still valuable evidence that a foreign jurisdiction recognises the
taxpayer as its resident — which speaks to the *Harding* "permanent place of abode" question — but
it does **not** engage any tie-breaker.

### Visa options

| Route | Requirement | Minimum stay | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Golden Visa (property)** | **AED 2m** (~AUD 830k) in approved real estate; mortgaged and off-plan property now qualifies (2026 change) | **None** | Best fit — but note it consumes ~70% of the taxpayer's net worth |
| **Golden Visa (skilled professional)** | Basic salary from AED 30,000/month + degree | None | Good if employment is available |
| **Golden Visa (entrepreneur)** | Approved project ≥ AED 500,000 | None | Possible |
| **Employment visa** | Company sponsorship | Tied to job | **Avoid an Australian-employer secondment — that is the *Quy* fact pattern** |
| **Freelance / remote work visa** | Income evidence | Varies | Weaker substance |

**Can the taxpayer become UAE tax resident without employment? Yes** — through the property
Golden Visa plus 183 days of presence and a permanent home. This is a genuine advantage over
Singapore and Hong Kong.

### Crypto practicalities

- **Regulators:** VARA (Dubai), ADGM/FSRA (Abu Dhabi), SCA (federal).
- **VARA-licensed venues:** Binance FZE (full VASP licence since April 2024), Kraken/Payward MENA,
  Bitpanda, Laser Digital, Rain, BitOasis, Hex Trust. Several support **native AED** deposits and
  withdrawals via local banks.
- **Banks used for crypto proceeds:** Emirates NBD, Mashreq, ADCB, WIO.
- **Does moving Bitcoin into the UAE have tax consequences?** **No.** There is no tax to trigger,
  and it does not change the Australian analysis either (I1 follows residency, not asset location).
- **Does selling through a UAE exchange change the analysis?** **No** for Australia. It helps
  practically — a VARA-licensed venue with AED banking rails is the cleanest path from BTC to
  spendable fiat.
- **Source of funds:** expect **extensive** documentation for a USD 1m+ liquidation — original
  purchase records, on-chain provenance for the full chain of custody, Australian tax returns
  (including the return showing the I1 gain declared), proof of residency, and often a
  Chainalysis/Elliptic-style wallet screening report. Budget **4–8 weeks** for onboarding, and
  liquidate in tranches through an OTC desk rather than a single market order.

### Weaknesses to be honest about

1. **No DTA — no tie-breaker.** If the ATO argues residency never ceased, there is **no treaty
   Article 4 tie-breaker** to resolve it. Domestic-law substance is the only defence. This is the
   UAE's single biggest weakness and it is material.
2. **The ATO knows this pattern.** *Quy* was a Dubai case. Expect scrutiny.
3. **Capital lock-up.** AED 2m of property is ~70% of the taxpayer's net worth, and would require
   selling Bitcoin — creating a chicken-and-egg problem addressed in the timeline at §5.
4. **Cost.** International school fees run AED 40,000–100,000+ per child per year; housing is
   expensive.

## 2.4 Singapore deep dive

**Verdict: 🟢 excellent tax and banking; 🟠 the residency pathway is the binding constraint.**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Personal capital gains tax | **None.** Singapore does not tax capital gains |
| Crypto held as investment | Not taxed — IRAS e-Tax Guide *Income Tax Treatment of Digital Tokens* |
| **Investment vs trading** | IRAS applies the **badges of trade**: frequency, holding period, profit motive, financing, reason for sale. A single disposal of a >12-month holding is capital. Frequent trading is revenue and taxable at up to 24% |
| Tax residency | **183 days** in a calendar year; administrative concessions where employment straddles two years or presence spans three consecutive calendar years |
| Tax residency certificate | Available from IRAS; **treaty-referable** because a DTA exists |
| Foreign-sourced gains | Not taxed (no CGT) |
| Remittance rules | Foreign-sourced **income** received in Singapore can be taxable for some categories, but **capital gains are simply outside the tax base** — remittance is irrelevant |
| Wealth tax | None |
| Departure tax | None |
| Estate tax | Abolished 2008 |
| **DTA with Australia** | **Yes — 1969 Agreement**, amended by protocols and by the MLI (synthesised text published 2021) |
| Information exchange | CRS since 2018; CARF committed |

### Residency: the problem

| Route | Threshold | Realistic for this taxpayer? |
|---|---|---|
| **Employment Pass** | ~SGD 5,000–5,600+/month, employer-sponsored | **Yes, if a genuine Singapore job is obtained** — this is the practical route |
| **ONE Pass** | SGD 30,000/month (~AUD 33k/month) | No |
| **Global Investor Programme** | **SGD 10 million** | No — far beyond the taxpayer's means |

A passive investor with AUD 1.2m **cannot buy** Singapore residency. They must be hired. And the
hire must be genuine and local — a secondment from an Australian employer reproduces *Quy*.

### Singapore vs UAE for this exact fact pattern

| | UAE | Singapore |
|---|---|---|
| Tax on the disposal | 0% | 0% |
| Residency without employment | **Yes** (Golden Visa) | **No** |
| Treaty tie-breaker with Australia | **No** | **Yes** |
| Badges-of-trade risk | Low (AED 1m business threshold) | Moderate (IRAS applies it actively) |
| Banking / crypto rails | Very good | **Best in class** (MAS-licensed: Coinbase, Independent Reserve, Crypto.com, DBS Digital Exchange) |
| Cost of living | High | Very high (housing especially) |
| Distance from Australia | ~13h | ~7–8h |

**Conclusion: Singapore is *superior* on treaty protection, banking and proximity, and *inferior*
on the one criterion that decides feasibility — whether this taxpayer can actually become
resident.** If the taxpayer can secure a genuine Singapore employment offer, Singapore arguably
becomes the better choice, because the treaty tie-breaker materially de-risks the biggest failure
mode. **That is the single most important "if" in this report.**

## 2.5 Hong Kong deep dive

**Verdict: 🟢 good tax; 🟠 no treaty; 🟡 residency requires a visa route.**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Territorial taxation | Yes — only Hong Kong-sourced profits are chargeable |
| Capital gains | **No capital gains tax at all** |
| Crypto investment gains | **0%** for genuine investors. IRD **DIPN 39** (*Profits Tax – Digital Economy, Electronic Commerce and Digital Assets*) confirms no CGT; profits tax applies only to trade or business profits sourced in Hong Kong |
| Trading vs investment | **Badges of trade.** High-frequency trading with significant capital may be a business → profits tax (15% for unincorporated) |
| Source rules | "What were the operations that produced the profits, and where did they take place" |
| Tax residency | No general residency-based taxation; residency matters mainly for treaty access |
| Offshore gains | Not taxed |
| Remittance | Irrelevant — no remittance basis for capital |
| Residency routes | Employment visa; **Top Talent Pass Scheme** (top-100 university graduate or high earner); Capital Investment Entrant Scheme (**HKD 30m**) |
| **DTA with Australia** | **None.** Only a Tax Information Exchange Agreement. Discussions about closer coordination remain preliminary as at 2026 |
| Departure taxation | None |
| Wealth / estate taxes | None (estate duty abolished 2006) |

Hong Kong's tax result is as good as the UAE's. It loses on: no treaty (same weakness as the
UAE); a harder residency pathway; slower bank onboarding; and, for a family planning a 5–10 year
stay, political and legal-system risk that a prudent adviser must at least name.

**Crypto rails:** SFC-licensed VATPs (OSL, HashKey, Bullish); ZA Bank is notably crypto-friendly
for fiat settlement.

## 2.6 Malaysia

**Verdict: 🟢 the best liveability-adjusted option; 🟡 some genuine legal uncertainty.**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Individual capital gains tax | **None** on crypto. Malaysia's CGT regime applies to companies disposing of unlisted shares, not to individuals' crypto |
| Crypto-specific rules | No dedicated CGT on digital assets for individuals. **LHDN will treat frequent, profit-motivated trading as business income** taxed at progressive rates to 30% |
| Foreign-source income | Exempt for resident individuals **to 31 December 2036** — **but conditionally**: the income must have been "subjected to tax of a similar character" in the source country |
| Tax residency | **182 days** in a calendar year |
| MM2H | Restructured 2024–25 into SEZ / Silver / Gold / Platinum tiers (fixed deposits from ~USD 32,000; Silver requires MYR 600,000 property + USD 150,000 deposit). **Sarawak S-MM2H** is cheaper: MYR 10,000/month income or MYR 100,000 liquid assets (single) / MYR 15,000 or MYR 200,000 (with dependants), MYR 500,000 fixed deposit, **30 days/year minimum stay**, minimum age 30 |
| Wealth tax | None |
| Exit tax | None |
| **DTA with Australia** | **Yes** |

### The two uncertainties, stated plainly

1. **Source characterisation.** Is a crypto gain realised by a Malaysian tax resident
   Malaysian-source or foreign-source? If **foreign-source**, the 2036 exemption applies — but it
   is conditional on the gain having been taxed abroad, which a 0%-taxed gain has not been. If
   **Malaysian-source**, the exemption is irrelevant and the answer turns on capital vs revenue —
   where a long-held investment should be capital and therefore untaxed. **Both routes plausibly
   arrive at 0%, by different reasoning.** That is comfortable but not certain.
2. **MM2H stay requirements are below the tax residency threshold.** MM2H's 30–90 day minimums do
   **not** make you a Malaysian tax resident. You need **182 days**. Plan to actually live there.

**Why Malaysia is nonetheless compelling for this family:** English-widely-spoken, excellent and
affordable international schools, cost of living perhaps a third of Sydney's, an 8-hour flight
home, the same time zone as Perth, and a DTA with Australia providing a tie-breaker. For a family
with a young child planning to return, it is the most *livable* option on the list.

**Crypto rails:** Securities Commission-registered Digital Asset Exchanges (Luno Malaysia,
Tokenize, MX Global, Hata). Liquidity for a **$3m** disposal is thinner than in Dubai or
Singapore — plan an OTC route or liquidate via a Singapore/UAE venue.

## 2.7 Thailand

**Verdict: 🟠 rules in flux; only viable via the LTR visa.**

- **Standard position:** Thai tax residents (180+ days) are taxed on foreign-source income
  **remitted** to Thailand. Departmental Instruction **Por 161/2566** (effective 1 January 2024)
  removed the old "remit in a later year and escape tax" planning; **Por 162/2566** grandfathered
  income earned before 1 January 2024.
- **The 2025 relaxation** — exempting foreign income remitted in the year it arises or the
  following year — was proposed by Royal Decree but, as at September 2026, **is still not
  enacted**. Reports indicate it was shelved ahead of the 2026 elections.
- **LTR visa** (Royal Decree No. 743, from 1 September 2022): the "Wealthy Global Citizen",
  "Wealthy Pensioner" and "Work-from-Thailand Professional" categories carry an **exemption for
  foreign income brought into Thailand**. Wealthy Global Citizen requires USD 1m in assets, USD
  80,000/yr income and USD 500,000 invested in Thailand.
- **Practical route:** simply **do not remit** the sale proceeds to Thailand. Keep them in the UAE
  or Singapore. That is lawful and effective, but it means Thailand is a place to *live*, not a
  place to *bank*.

Thailand has a DTA with Australia and is cheap and pleasant, but the legislative instability
around remittance taxation is a real strike against a 5–10 year plan.

## 2.8 Practical banking and liquidation — top 5 jurisdictions

Liquidating AUD 1m–10m of Bitcoin is an operational problem as much as a tax one.

| | UAE | Singapore | Hong Kong | Malaysia | Switzerland |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Regulated venues** | VARA/ADGM: Binance FZE, Kraken MENA, Bitpanda, Rain, BitOasis, Hex Trust | MAS DPT: Coinbase, Independent Reserve, Crypto.com, DBS Digital Exchange, Sygnum | SFC VATPs: OSL, HashKey, Bullish | SC DAX: Luno, Tokenize, MX Global, Hata | Bitcoin Suisse, Sygnum, AMINA |
| **OTC desks for $1m+** | Deep | Deep | Good | Thin | Good |
| **Bank acceptance of crypto proceeds** | Good (Emirates NBD, Mashreq, ADCB, WIO) | Good (DBS, OCBC, UOB) | Slow; ZA Bank is the exception | Moderate | Excellent, with documentation |
| **Source-of-funds burden** | Heavy | Heavy | Heavy | Moderate | Heaviest |
| **Realistic onboarding time** | 4–8 weeks | 4–8 weeks | 6–12 weeks | 3–6 weeks | 8–16 weeks |
| **Local fiat pair** | AED | SGD | HKD (limited) | MYR | CHF |

### Universal practical rules

1. **Onboard before you need to sell.** Open exchange and bank accounts and complete KYC **months**
   ahead. A frozen account at the moment of sale is the most likely practical failure.
2. **Self-custody history is an asset, not a liability** — provided you can document it. Coins
   arriving from a long-dormant self-custody address with a clean provenance chain and matching
   original purchase records are *easier* to clear than coins arriving from a mixer, a
   privacy-focused chain, or a sanctioned or high-risk venue.
3. **Expect large transfers to be held pending compliance review.** Send a small test transaction,
   then tranches. Do not send the full position in one movement.
4. **Assemble a source-of-funds pack in advance:** original exchange purchase records; bank
   statements showing the AUD that bought the coins; the full on-chain custody chain; Australian
   tax returns (especially the departure-year return declaring the I1 gain); the departure
   documentation set; proof of new residency; and a third-party wallet screening report.
5. **Never use mixers, coin-join, or transaction-obfuscation tools.** They will not reduce tax by
   one cent and they will destroy your ability to bank the proceeds — and could expose you to
   AML offences.
6. **Liquidate in tranches through an OTC desk.** A $3m market order moves the book and looks
   anomalous to compliance systems.

### ⚠️ A warning about the ranking function itself

The brief's weights give the **destination-country CGT rate only 20%**. That is too little for a
strategy whose entire purpose is to have the disposal fall in a 0% jurisdiction. The artefact is
visible in the table: **New Zealand scores 6.50 — mid-table — despite taxing the disposal as
ordinary income at up to 39%**, which would cost roughly **$1.09m** on a $2.8m gain and defeat the
whole exercise. Cyprus (8% from 2026) and Andorra (10%) score similarly misleadingly.

**Treat criterion 2 as a gate, not a weighted factor.** Any jurisdiction that taxes the disposal at
more than a few per cent should be eliminated before scoring, regardless of how well it scores
elsewhere. The rows marked ⚠️ above fail that gate and are shown only to demonstrate the point.

### The five

**#1 — United Arab Emirates (8.45).** The only jurisdiction that combines a genuine statutory 0%
on personal capital gains, a residency pathway that does **not** require employment, and mature
crypto liquidation infrastructure. Its weakness — no DTA with Australia — is real, and is managed
by making the domestic-law residency position overwhelming rather than by relying on a treaty.

**#2 — Malta (7.90).** 0% on foreign-source capital gains for non-domiciled residents even when
remitted; a DTA with Australia in force since 1985 providing a tie-breaker; EU legal stability; no
wealth or exit tax. Held back by genuinely difficult banking for crypto proceeds, the €15,000
minimum annual tax under GRP/TRP, and the need to be careful that the gain is characterised as
**foreign**-source (use a non-Maltese exchange and a non-Maltese bank).

**#3= — Singapore (7.85).** Best-in-class banking, no CGT, a DTA, and only 7–8 hours from home.
**Ranked third solely because the taxpayer probably cannot get in.** If a genuine Singapore
employment offer is available, promote it to **#1** — the treaty tie-breaker is worth more than
anything the UAE offers.

**#3= — Malaysia (7.85).** The best real-life fit for a family with a young child intending to
return: affordable, English-speaking, good schools, same time zone, DTA in force. Marked down for
uncertainty about the source characterisation of crypto gains and the conditionality of the
foreign-source exemption.

**#5 — Switzerland (7.75).** The strongest treaty position and excellent private banking, and
genuine 0% under the Circular 36 private-wealth-management safe harbour. Held back by an annual
**wealth tax** on the full Bitcoin value (~0.1–1%, i.e. roughly $3,000–$30,000 per year on a $3m
position), a residency pathway that for a non-EU/EFTA national means a quota work permit or a
lump-sum *forfait* costing more than the entire Australian departure tax, and a very high cost of
living. Also note the Circular 36 "no borrowed capital" criterion, which rules out BTC-backed
lending.
