# 5 — Recommended strategy, return planning and timeline

Covers brief sections 13, 14 and 29.

## 5.1 Temporary vs permanent relocation (§13)

Three scenarios, modelled on identical financial facts.

| | **Scenario 1** Permanent emigration | **Scenario 2** 5 years abroad, then return | **Scenario 3** 10 years abroad, then return |
|---|---|---|---|
| CGT event I1 at departure | Yes — $192,400 | Yes — $192,400 | Yes — $192,400 |
| Australian tax on the sale abroad | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Australian tax on return | n/a | **$0** | **$0** |
| **Total Australian tax** | **$192,400** | **$192,400** | **$192,400** |
| Statutory difference | — | **None** | **None** |
| Evidential strength of the residency position | Strongest | **Defensible with the right facts** | Very strong |
| Main residence exemption "life events" test still available (≤6 years abroad) | n/a | **Yes** | **No** |
| Practical audit risk | Low | **Moderate** | Low |

### The legal answer, and the practical one

**Legally, the result does not differ at all.** Australia has **no temporary non-residence rule**.
Contrast the UK, where FA 2013 Sch 45 Pt 4 claws back gains realised during an absence of five
years or less by anyone UK-resident in four of the seven prior years. Australia has nothing
equivalent: s 855‑10 disregards the gain of a foreign resident on a non-TAP asset, and s 855‑45
resets the cost base on return, with no lookback and no clawback.

**Practically, duration is evidence.** Nobody has to prove they intended to stay away forever, but
a taxpayer who leaves, sells a large asset, and returns 18 months later invites the inference that
residency never truly ceased — the *Quy* inference. Duration is not a legal test, but it is
powerful circumstantial evidence of the intention that *is* a legal test.

**Practical guidance:**

- **Under 2 years: 🔴** Do not attempt. The optics are indefensible and the ATO will press hard.
- **2–4 years: 🟠** Possible only with overwhelming substance — family fully relocated, Australian
  home sold, foreign home purchased, foreign employment, minimal Australian visits — and only if
  the return is driven by a genuine, documentable change in circumstances.
- **5–7 years: 🟡→🟢** The brief's central case. Defensible with the right facts. Note that the
  main residence "life events" exception requires foreign residency of **6 years or less**, so
  there is a modest reason to keep an absence at or under six years if an Australian home is
  retained.
- **8–10+ years: 🟢** Very strong. At this duration the residency argument is close to unarguable
  on ordinary facts, and a return is plainly a fresh migration.

**One thing to be careful about:** do not manufacture a stated intention you do not hold. If the
honest position is "we intend to live overseas indefinitely and will reassess", write that down.
If it is "we intend to be back in five years", that is more difficult but not fatal — plenty of
people genuinely emigrate and later change their minds, and *Harding* itself involved a taxpayer
who had previously returned to Australia and left again. What sinks a case is a **fixed, documented
return date paired with an Australian home and family kept warm** — the *Quy* pattern.

## 5.2 The return strategy (§14)

What should happen **before** returning to Australia? The options in the brief, with Australian
consequences.

| Option | What happens | Australian tax consequence | Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Sell BTC while non-resident, hold cash overseas** | Gain disregarded (s 855‑10). Cash is a foreign currency CGT asset; s 855‑45 resets its cost base to AUD market value on the day residency resumes | **$0 on the gain.** Only Div 775 forex movement between resumption and remittance is assessable, plus interest earned after resumption | 🟢 **Best** |
| **B — Sell while non-resident, invest proceeds offshore** | Same as A; the new investments get a s 855‑45 market-value cost base on re-entry | **$0 on the BTC gain.** Only post-return growth on the new investments is taxable | 🟢 **Best** |
| **C — Sell while non-resident, buy shares/ETFs offshore** | Same as B. Note that on re-entry the **12-month clock restarts**, so hold 12+ months after return before selling to get the 50% discount | **$0 on the BTC gain.** Watch out for foreign investment funds and any distributions after resumption | 🟢 |
| **D — Sell while non-resident, buy Australian assets** | Australian real property becomes **TAP** immediately; gains are Australian-taxable regardless of residency, and the discount is **apportioned** for the foreign-resident holding days under s 115‑115 | **$0 on the BTC gain, but the new Australian asset carries a permanently degraded discount.** Also exposes you to foreign resident CGT withholding and, if it is a dwelling, the main residence exemption denial | 🟠 **Buy Australian assets *after* returning, not before** |
| **E — Bring cash into Australia after becoming resident** | Remittance of capital | **Not a taxable event.** Australia has **no remittance basis**. A forex gain or loss can arise under Div 775 on movement between the resumption date and the transfer | 🟢 |
| **F — Become resident again while holding foreign cash** | s 855‑45 resets the foreign currency's cost base to market value on the resumption date | **$0.** Only subsequent currency movement is in scope | 🟢 |
| **G — Return holding appreciated non-Australian investments** | s 855‑45 deems acquisition at market value on the resumption date, and restarts the discount clock | **$0 on all pre-return appreciation.** This is the most valuable feature of Australia's re-entry rules | 🟢 |

### Does transferring sale proceeds from an overseas account to an Australian account create a taxable event?

**No.** This is the question most often asked and most often misunderstood.

- Australia taxes **income and capital gains**, not remittances. There is no remittance basis for
  Australian residents. Moving your own money between your own accounts is not a CGT event, not
  ordinary income, and not a "deemed" anything.
- What **can** arise is a **forex realisation gain or loss under Division 775** — but only on
  currency movement occurring **after** you resume Australian residency. If you become a resident
  when USD/AUD is at one level and convert at another, the difference on the foreign-currency
  right is assessable or deductible. On $3m, a 5% currency move is a $150,000 forex item, so this
  is not trivial: consider converting to AUD **before** resuming residency, which crystallises the
  currency position outside the Australian net.
- **Reporting is a separate matter from taxation.** Transfers of AUD 10,000 or more are reported to
  AUSTRAC as international funds transfer instructions and the ATO sees them. Expect the transfer
  to be noticed. That is fine — it is fully explicable, and the documentation file exists for
  exactly this purpose.

**Recommended sequence:** sell the Bitcoin while non-resident → convert to AUD (or to the currency
you will hold long term) **while still non-resident** → hold in a plain personal bank account →
resume Australian residency → remit at leisure. **Do not** route the proceeds through an offshore
trust or company: s 99B ITAA 1936, TD 2024/9 and PCG 2024/3 can assess a returning Australian
beneficiary on trust corpus, loans, gifts and the use of trust property. A plain personal bank
account is both simpler and far safer.

## 5.3 The recommended strategy, as a numbered sequence

1. **Engage an Australian international-tax specialist and a UAE (or destination) tax adviser
   before doing anything.** Put the 20 questions in §6.4 to them.
2. **Decide the destination on family viability first, tax second** — the tax outcome is 0% in all
   the shortlisted jurisdictions, so the differentiator is whether the family can actually live
   there for 5+ years.
3. **Clean up the Bitcoin position while still resident.** Reconcile every parcel; document
   acquisition dates, quantities, AUD costs and exchange-rate sources; consolidate into wallets
   you control. **Do not** wrap, swap, bridge, lend into DeFi, or convert to stablecoins.
4. **Deal with the Australian home before ceasing residency** — sell it as a resident if it holds
   significant gain, because the main residence exemption is lost entirely for a foreign-resident
   vendor.
5. **Harvest genuine capital losses** in the departure year (no wash sales), remembering each
   dollar is worth ~22.6c against a discounted gain.
6. **Make carry-forward concessional super contributions** in the departure year if the cash is
   available and the 26-year lock-up is acceptable (≈$29,750 permanent saving).
7. **End Australian employment before 30 June**, so the departure year carries no Australian salary.
8. **Move the entire family in early July**, at the start of the Australian income year.
9. **Establish real substance abroad immediately** — long lease or purchase, school enrolment, local
   bank, local licence, local health cover, community ties.
10. **Do not make the s 104‑165 choice.** Report CGT event I1 in the final Australian return, pay
    the ≈$192,400 (funded by a pre-departure partial sale of the oldest, highest-cost-base parcels,
    or a low-LTV BTC-backed loan).
11. **Obtain a foreign tax residency certificate for every year abroad**, without exception.
12. **Hold the Bitcoin.** No Australian CGT event can occur while you are a foreign resident holding
    a non-TAP asset.
13. **Sell in year 5–7 while genuinely non-resident**, through a regulated venue in the destination,
    in tranches, with the source-of-funds pack ready.
14. **Convert to your long-term currency while still non-resident**, then hold personally — no
    trusts, no companies.
15. **Return to Australia only after the sale has settled and at least one full foreign tax year has
    passed since it**, with the market value of every remaining asset documented at the resumption
    date for the s 855‑45 reset.

## 5.5 What the taxpayer actually buys for $192,400

Stated plainly, so the decision is clear-eyed:

**Bought:** permanent exclusion of all post-departure Bitcoin appreciation from the Australian tax
net; a $462,400 advantage over staying in Australia on the base-case numbers, rising to $2.1m if
Bitcoin reaches $10m; and a clean, defensible, fully disclosed position.

**Paid, beyond the $192,400:** relocation costs; international school fees (AED 40,000–100,000+ per
child per year in the UAE); the loss of a $150,000 Australian salary while a foreign job is
found; professional fees on both sides likely in the $20,000–$60,000 range; loss of Medicare;
a spouse's career disruption; distance from family; and five to ten years of a life lived
somewhere the family did not otherwise choose.

**If the family would not move for any reason other than tax, the strategy is both worse value than
it looks and legally more fragile than it looks.** The $462,400 advantage is real, but it is not
life-changing against those costs, and the residency position is strongest precisely when the move
is genuinely wanted. **Only do this if you would be reasonably happy to have done it even if the
tax result were neutral.**
